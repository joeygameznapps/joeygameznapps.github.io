Overview

Countdowns is an app for iPhone and iPad that counts down to the days that matter. It has no accounts, no sign-in and no servers of its own: your countdowns live on your device and, if you use iCloud, in your own private iCloud storage. They never come to us.

The only third party that receives data from the app is Google AdMob, which serves the banner ad that keeps Countdowns free. Ads can only be personalised if you allow tracking when the app asks. If you choose not to, the app never receives your device's advertising identifier and the ads are chosen from context alone. If you buy the optional Customization upgrade, ads are removed completely and Google's ad software is no longer started.

Contact and support

Questions, problems, feature requests or anything about your data: Business@joemet123.com. Put "Countdowns" in the subject and you will have a reply within 30 days. Joemet123 LLC is the data controller for this app.

What the app stores

Your countdowns (their titles, dates and times, notes, collections, colours, themes and icons) and your settings are saved on your device using Apple's standard on-device storage. The app's own widgets read them from the same device so they can show your countdowns on your Home Screen and Lock Screen.

If you are signed in to iCloud, your countdowns also sync between your devices through your private iCloud database, using Apple's CloudKit. That data belongs to your Apple Account and is handled under Apple's Privacy Policy; we cannot see or access it.

Deleting the app removes its data from that device. Copies kept in iCloud can be managed or deleted in iOS Settings, under your name, then iCloud. The app does not ask you to sign in and does not collect your name, email address, contacts, calendar, microphone, camera or precise location.

Photos

The app never asks for access to your photo library. If you choose a photo as a countdown's icon, iOS's photo picker hands the app only that one photo. It is shrunk to a small image and saved with the countdown, on your device and in your iCloud like the rest of your countdowns.

Reminders

If you turn on a reminder, the app asks for permission to send notifications, and iOS schedules the reminder on your device. Nothing about it is sent to us.

Backups

"Export countdowns" creates a file that you save wherever you choose, and "Import countdowns" reads a file that you pick. We never receive these files.

Advertising (Google AdMob)

The app shows one banner ad, served by Google AdMob, a service of Google LLC. To serve and measure that ad, Google's software in the app may collect:

  • IP address, and an approximate, city-level location derived from it;
  • device model, operating system version and language;
  • whether an ad was shown, seen or tapped;
  • diagnostic and performance information about ad delivery;
  • your device's advertising identifier (IDFA), but only if you allow tracking.

Google uses this to choose and measure ads and to detect fraud and invalid traffic. How Google handles it is described in Google's Privacy Policy and in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services. Your countdowns, notes and photos are never given to the ad software.

Tracking permission and consent

The first time you open the app, a short explanation from Google is followed by Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt (unless you own Customization, which removes ads). If you allow tracking, Google may use your advertising identifier to personalise and measure ads. If you ask the app not to track, it never receives that identifier and ads are contextual. You can change your answer at any time in iOS Settings, under Privacy & Security, then Tracking.

In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, Google's consent form is shown before any ad is requested, and you can reopen it at any time from the "Privacy choices" button in the app's Settings. The app also includes Apple's SKAdNetwork identifiers, which let ad networks count installs that came from an ad without identifying you.

Purchases (Apple)

The app sells exactly one thing: an optional, one-time "Customization" purchase that removes all ads and unlocks custom colours, holiday themes and photo icons. Once you own it, the banner ad disappears straight away, and from then on the app does not start Google's ad software or show its consent form or the tracking prompt, so nothing further is sent to Google AdMob. It is sold through Apple's in-app purchase system. Apple processes the payment and holds your payment details; the app only receives Apple's confirmation that the purchase completed. Purchases are governed by Apple's Privacy Policy and the App Store terms, and "Restore purchases" re-checks with Apple on a new device.

Children

Countdowns is a general-audience app and is not directed to children. We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, of any age: the app has no accounts and no way to send us anything from inside it. If you believe a child has provided personal information, contact us at the address above and we will help remove it.

Your choices and rights

You control your countdowns directly: edit or delete them in the app, delete the app, or remove its iCloud data in iOS Settings. You can turn tracking off in iOS Settings at any time, and in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland the "Privacy choices" button reopens the consent form. For data processed by Google AdMob, you can exercise your rights with Google as described in its privacy policy. If you live in a region with data protection rights such as access, correction or deletion, write to us at the address above and we will help.

Security and retention

We operate no servers and hold no database, so there is nothing for us to retain, breach or hand over. Apple keeps your iCloud data for as long as you keep it in your account, and Google retains advertising data under its own policies.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted at this address with an updated effective date. If the app ever begins to collect something it does not collect today, that change will appear here and in the App Store's own privacy labels before it ships.
